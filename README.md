# Pennsylvania SB 992 Telemarketing Compliance Kit

**$197 digital product — October 2026 edition.**

Everything a Pennsylvania business that calls or texts customers needs to act on Act 47 of 2026 (SB 992) — the rewrite of the Telemarketer Registration Act — without hiring a law firm to do the research first. The Act takes effect **October 19, 2026**; this kit is designed to get you compliant **by October 18**.

> **DISCLAIMER — NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Every file in this kit is general information, not legal advice, and creates no attorney-client relationship. Telemarketing law is fact-specific; have a licensed Pennsylvania attorney review your practices before you rely on anything here. Where reputable sources disagree or the statute's text is silent, this kit says so in **[UNVERIFIED]** and **[NOT IN TEXT]** flags rather than guessing.

## Who it's for

Pennsylvania business owners, sales and marketing leads, and agencies whose teams call, text, or leave voicemails for customers and prospects — including AI/agent voice callers — and who need the new rules operationalized: consent language, opt-out handling, calling hours, do-not-call hygiene, and the telemarketer-registration determination.

## What the research established (and what it did not)

Verified from the enrolled text: the expanded "telephone solicitation" definition (calls, voicemail, ringless voicemail, texts; residential, business, wireless); the four prior-express-written-consent criteria; the robocall consent prohibition; quiet hours (no Sundays, nothing before 9 a.m. or after 7 p.m., nothing on legal holidays); the 7 text opt-out keywords; quarterly do-not-call list acquisition with a 30-day effectiveness lag; registration 30 days in advance with 13 exemption categories; caller-ID and synthetic-media fraud rules.

Flagged honestly in the kit: SB 992 contains **no dollar penalties of its own** — civil penalties (up to $1,000 per violation, $3,000 if the victim is 60+) arise under the UTPCPL through the Act's § 6(a) and require willfulness; the $50,000 bond / $500 biennial fee / failure-to-register misdemeanor come from the underlying Act (§ 2244), which SB 992 does not amend — **[UNVERIFIED]** against the post-Act 47 consolidation; the Act states **no** text opt-out honor deadline and **no** AI-disclosure duty — **[NOT IN TEXT]**.
